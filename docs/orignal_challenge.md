THREADWISE — Product Requirements Document
A problem-led brief for open-ended product thinking
1. The Challenge
People often manage work through a mix of meetings, conversations, clients, and follow-ups. Important actions can get buried across meeting notes, chats, emails, and personal to-do lists.

As the number of ongoing relationships grows, it becomes difficult to remember who needs a follow-up, what was discussed, what was promised, what needs to happen next, and which conversations are becoming inactive.

The challenge is to make this process simpler and more reliable without creating another complicated project-management tool.
2. The Idea
Threadwise is a lightweight concept for organizing professional relationships and the work that comes out of them.

The core concept is a thread: a continuing relationship or conversation around a person, client, project, opportunity, or another meaningful context.

A thread should help a user retain enough context to understand the relationship and recognize what needs attention next.
3. Target Users
The product is intended for people who frequently manage work through relationships, conversations, and follow-ups.
- Product managers
- Founders
- Consultants
- Account managers
- Freelancers
Participants may choose one specific audience or design for a broader group.
4. Core Goal
Help users avoid losing track of an important professional conversation or the work that follows from it.
5. Basic Requirements
The solution should provide a way for users to:
- Create and manage a thread.
- Associate people with a thread.
- Keep a people directory associated with the user.
- Capture relevant context.
- Keep track of things that need to be done.
- Understand what requires their attention.
These requirements describe the outcome, not the implementation. Participants should decide how each capability should work.
6. Constraints
- Keep the experience simple and easy to understand.
- Keep the product relationship-focused and action-oriented.
- Design for a user managing approximately 10 to 50 ongoing threads.
- Do not turn the product into a full project-management suite.
- Prioritize a coherent core experience over a large number of features.
7. What Is Intentionally Open
There is no prescribed interface or user flow. Participants are expected to make their own product decisions.
- What a thread looks like
- What information belongs inside a thread
- How tasks or follow-ups are represented
- How users discover what needs attention
- What the home or starting screen should show
- How people and tasks should connect
- How reminders or follow-ups should work
- What the simplest useful workflow looks like
8. Success Criteria
A strong submission should demonstrate:
- Clear understanding of the underlying problem.
- A focused solution rather than a collection of unrelated features.
- Thoughtful product and UX decisions.
- A simple path to completing the core job.
- A working prototype that communicates the proposed experience.
9. Deliverable
Build a working prototype that demonstrates your interpretation of the problem.

The prototype does not need to solve every possible use case. It should make a clear product argument through the experience you choose to build.
10. Final Prompt
How might we help people never lose track of an important professional conversation or the work that follows from it?
Interpretation boundary: Any feature, technology, persona prioritization, or numerical success metric outside the text above is our proposal, not a mandated hackathon requirement.
